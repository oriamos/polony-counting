## Implementation deviations / status (after the scientific review, 2026-10-10)

What the app implements, where it deviates from the recommendations below, and what is still open.

1. **Debris and yellow.** `debris_like` (scale ratio >= 1) is queued for review (it has a review priority
   between `saturated`/`weak_signal` and `dense_cluster`). A matched red+green pair that is debris-like in
   BOTH channels is NOT auto-classed yellow: it becomes `uncertain` with `debris_like` and a history note
   (on test_img: 22 yellow before -> 0 yellow / 21 uncertain after). One debris channel: stays yellow, queued;
   single-channel objects keep their class (flag only). Compact debris is still not removed or moved to a
   "rejected candidates" layer (deviation from §3 recommendation); the 0.3-scale recall/precision numbers of §2.4 are
   not reproduced by the app.
2. **Concentration** uses the lab formula `abundance = mean(field counts) * (field_area / grid_area) / sample_volume_ul * 1000`
   (no concentration factor, as in the T7 sheets; see [LAB_EXPORT.md](LAB_EXPORT.md)); `sample_total_area_px2` is no longer
   used. `concentration_formula_confirmed` means "field geometry and series mapping confirmed". No CI; not validated against the
   researcher's own numbers.
3. **Saturated plateaus** are merged into ONE spot at the plateau centroid (all spots within the connected
   saturated component dilated by the spot radius); a plateau with equivalent diameter > 6 spot radii also gets
   `suspicious_shape`. Plateau size is a lower bound of the true spot; its centroid is not a sub-pixel estimate of
   the true centre. Several touching saturated polonies cannot be resolved: a plateau that is elongated (> 1.3) or
   carried more than one LoG maximum also gets `suspicious_shape` (merge kept, no splitting). SNR is undefined for
   merged plateaus, so `weak_signal` / `near_threshold` are skipped for them.
4. **Masking** follows §1.4: LoG on the real background-corrected image, response zeroed outside the mask, maxima
   inside the mask (+1 px, the final decision uses the refined centre), automatic threshold from maxima strictly
   inside the mask. Only pixels > 4 sigma from the mask and saturated pixels outside the mask are median-filled.
   Synthetic border test: spots centred at x >= 100 outside a region [0,100) are not counted; inside positions
   are exact to <= 0.05 px.
5. **Preview == full run:** background correction on the full image, automatic threshold (in noise units) and noise sigma from the full masked
   image, cached (small LRU keyed by image hash + mask + radius/min-distance/background). Tiling already shared
   one global threshold. The LoG/measurement of a preview runs on a padded crop.
6. **One geometry rule** (`detection/masks.py`, half-open pixel-centre rule) is used by detection, review
   membership and areas (a 10x10 rectangle has area 100; a point on the low edge is inside, on the high edge
   outside). `review/geometry.py` delegates. Polygon vertices are not snapped; areas are pixel-centre counts, not
   polygon areas (a 316.23 px square counts 317 x 317 pixel centres).
7. **Overlapping counting regions:** first listed region wins for membership AND area (area is net of earlier
   regions), so region areas add up to the union area.
8. **Alignment strip:** the part of the green channel uncovered by an applied integer shift (zeros) is excluded
   from green detection and neutralised before the LoG (`run_detection(..., alignment=(dy, dx))`). Red-only
   objects in that strip cannot be matched and are not flagged separately (a note gives the strip area).
9. **Background:** saturated raw pixels get weight 0 in the gaussian background estimate (also without a mask).
   `white_tophat` has no such rule. The `processed_*` image views are computed without the counting mask.
10. **SNR** peak = background-corrected image interpolated at the refined centre (not the 3x3 maximum, which
    gave SNR ~ +1.5 for pure noise). Synthetic check: true A/sigma = 0 -> median SNR ~0.1; A/sigma = 5 -> ~4.8.
    SNR is still a single-pixel value vs per-pixel noise, not a matched-filter detection statistic.
11. **Matching:** alternative partners are searched out to tolerance + max(1.5 px, 25 %): a 5.9 px pair with a 6.1 px
    alternative is `ambiguous_match`. The assignment is one-to-one per spot; this does NOT prove that two spots
    are different physical objects or that one object was not split into two spots.
12. **Thresholds are in noise units** (LoG response / 1.4826 x MAD of the signed LoG response over valid pixels).
    Big-FISH's elbow search uses an absolute candidate grid, so the previous automatic threshold depended on image
    brightness (see "Measured performance and limits"). An elbow below 5 noise units is treated as the noise regime
    (fallback to 5, WARNING note), and a channel with > 50 % debris-like spots gets a WARNING note.
13. **Still open / not validated:** accuracy beyond the 8 slides of REAL_DATA_VALIDATION.md (one annotator, one scan session);
    green and yellow performance on real green polonies (test_img has none; on the 8 slides the green page is nearly empty); the default review thresholds (weak_snr 5, elongation 1.8, debris ratio 1.0)
    are starting points; concentration formula and chamber geometry await the researcher; no confidence intervals.

### Measured performance and limits (test_img.tif, red channel only, 15 px match to the 77 embedded marks, 17 counted grid squares)

* Defaults on the single red-only image: recall vs the 77 marks about 0.53-0.60 and precision 0.19-0.42 for all red-detected spots
  (R = 3: 185 spots, recall 0.53, precision 0.22; R = 4: 124, 0.53, 0.33; R = 5: 103, 0.56, 0.42, after the noise-unit change; before it,
  at R = 3 the numbers were 244, 0.60, 0.19). The marks are imprecise and were made by one annotator; the parameters were explored on this same image.
* There is no green and no yellow validation: the image has no real green polonies.
* The automatic threshold was brightness dependent (R = 5: 103 / 67 / 64 detections, recall 0.56 / 0.44 / 0.43 at brightness x1 / x0.5 / x0.25);
  it is now noise-normalised and gives identical detections at those scales (R = 3, 4, 5).
* Counts are not validated for accuracy. The app reports detections plus review flags, not an accuracy-checked count.
* The debris criterion is relative to the configured radius; touching saturated polonies stay merged (flagged); an empty or very sparse channel
  can have its automatic threshold in the noise regime (WARNING).

## 1. Verified Big-FISH API (big-fish 0.6.2, read from installed source + tested)

Source read: `lib/python3.13/site-packages/bigfish/` of the project virtualenv, files `bigfish/detection/{spot_detection,dense_decomposition,utils}.py`,
`bigfish/stack/{filter,preprocess}.py`. Mechanics checked by `scripts/experiments/00_bigfish_api_check.py`
(that script uses a synthetic image **only to test the API mechanics**, never accuracy).

### 1.1 `detect_spots` signature and unit mapping
```python
bigfish.detection.detect_spots(images, threshold=None, remove_duplicate=True,
    return_threshold=False, voxel_size=None, spot_radius=None,
    log_kernel_size=None, minimum_distance=None)
```
* You must give **either** `(voxel_size, spot_radius)` **or** `(log_kernel_size, minimum_distance)`
  (if all four are given, the pixel pair wins). Anything else raises `ValueError`.
* With `(voxel_size, spot_radius)`, Big-FISH computes `log_kernel_size = minimum_distance = spot_radius / voxel_size`
  per axis (`utils.get_object_radius_pixel`: `b / a`). The "nm" label is only a ratio. So
  `voxel_size=1, spot_radius=r` is **exactly** `log_kernel_size=r, minimum_distance=r` (asserted in the script).
* **Use the pixel pair** (`log_kernel_size`, `minimum_distance`) in the app. Never pass a fabricated
  voxel size in nm. `log_kernel_size` is the **standard deviation (sigma) of the Gaussian in the LoG**, in pixels,
  not a radius and not a diameter. `minimum_distance` is in pixels.
* `minimum_distance` → local maxima via `ndi.maximum_filter(size=2*ceil(md)+1)`: a **square** (Chebyshev) window,
  so two peaks closer than `ceil(md)` along both axes collapse into one; diagonal neighbours up to `ceil(md)*sqrt(2)` too.
* `threshold` is applied to the **LoG-filtered image** (`stack.log_filter`: `-gaussian_laplace(img, sigma)`, negatives
  clipped to 0), **not** to raw intensity. The LoG is **not scale-normalised**: a Gaussian blob of amplitude `A` and
  sigma `s` filtered at sigma `t` gives a peak ≈ `2·A·s²/(s²+t²)²`. Example: `A=2000, s=t=5` → ≈40. Hence
  thresholds are not comparable across `log_kernel_size` values or between channels; the UI must label it
  "LoG response threshold", and `threshold_scale` multiplies that LoG-unit value.
* `threshold` must be Python `int`/`float`/`None`; `np.float32(…)` is **rejected** (`TypeError` from
  `check_parameter`). `np.float64` passes (it subclasses `float`). Always wrap: `float(thr)`.
* Returned spots: `np.int64` array `(n, 2)` in **(row, col) = (y, x)** order. With `remove_duplicate=True`,
  plateau maxima are merged by connected component and the centroid is **truncated** with `astype(int64)`
  (not rounded): up to 1 px bias toward the origin. Sub-pixel position is not provided; refine yourself if needed.
* If a **list** of images is passed, ONE threshold is computed from all of them pooled. Never pass red and green
  together; run each channel separately.

### 1.2 Input dtypes (pitfalls)
* Accepted: `uint8, uint16, float32, float64`, 2-D or 3-D. `int32`, `float16`, etc. → `TypeError`.
* `uint16` path: image is scaled to [0,1] float64, LoG'd, then re-cast with `img_as_uint` (×65535) → the LoG output
  is **re-quantised to integers**. For broad blobs (sigma 5 px) the LoG peak of a 1000-count polony is ~20 LoG
  units, so integer quantisation is coarse and the threshold candidates are integer steps.
* `float32/float64` path: no rescaling; LoG in the image's own units. **Recommended: pass the background-corrected
  `float32` image** (what `correct_background` returns), never a `uint16` after background subtraction
  (subtraction in uint16 wraps around below 0).
* Big-FISH `stack.remove_background_gaussian` subtracts in the input dtype with `np.subtract(..., out=zeros, where=img>bg)`:
  fine for floats, but do our own background correction in float32 (see Recommended defaults).

### 1.3 What the automated threshold does
`_detect_spots_from_images` when `threshold=None`:
1. Candidate thresholds: `0 .. int(percentile(LoG, 99.9999))`, **integer steps** if that end value ≥ 100, otherwise
   100 values of `linspace`. (Note: it builds a Python `list` of every LoG pixel value: ~3.5 M floats for a
   2450×1438 image; slow and memory-hungry but works.)
2. For each candidate, `log(count of local maxima with LoG > t)`, smoothed by a centred moving average (n=5); the
   tail where `log(count) <= 2` (fewer than ~8 maxima) is dropped.
3. `get_breaking_point`: the elbow = first point after the initial steep drop where the gradient comes back to the
   mean slope. It assumes a curve with a fast-decaying noise part and a plateau of real spots.

When it fails (all observed in our tests):
* **Few true spots** (sparse plates: T7A rows have 0–7 per field) → no plateau; the elbow lands in the noise.
  Pure-noise image: auto threshold = **0.0** and ~300 "spots" on a 300×300 tile.
* **Fewer than ~8 maxima above 0 after masking** or a monotone curve → `get_breaking_point` raises
  `ValueError: False is not in list` (`m.index(False)`). In `detect_spots` an empty curve leaves `threshold=None`
  → zero spots + `UserWarning`. The engine must catch both and report "auto threshold unavailable".
* **Bright structures that are not spots** (rim glare, the saturated area outside the chamber, worm-like artefacts)
  extend the high-intensity tail; the percentile end and the curve shape depend on what is inside the analysed area,
  so **the mask must be applied before the threshold is chosen** (below).
* Different images → different thresholds even at the same settings; the auto value is a starting point, never a calibration.

### 1.4 How to pass a mask (Big-FISH has no mask argument)
Use the decomposed API so that both the threshold choice and the detection only see the valid area:
```python
import numpy as np
import bigfish.stack as stack
import bigfish.detection as det

def bigfish_detect_px(img_f32, valid_mask, sigma_px, min_dist_px, threshold=None, scale=1.0):
    """img_f32: background-corrected float32 2-D image; valid_mask: bool, same shape.
    Returns (yx int64 array, threshold_used: float|None, note: str)."""
    log = stack.log_filter(img_f32.astype(np.float32, copy=False), float(sigma_px))  # LoG on the FULL image:
    lmax = det.local_maximum_detection(log, float(min_dist_px))                     # no artificial mask edges
    log_m = np.where(valid_mask, log, 0).astype(log.dtype)                           # zero outside mask
    lmax_m = lmax & valid_mask
    note = ""
    if threshold is None:
        try:
            threshold = det.automated_threshold_setting(log_m, lmax_m)
        except ValueError as e:  # get_breaking_point on short/monotone curve
            return np.empty((0, 2), np.int64), None, f"auto threshold failed: {e}"
        if threshold is None:
            return np.empty((0, 2), np.int64), None, "auto threshold failed: no candidate maxima"
        threshold = float(threshold) * float(scale)
    spots, _ = det.spots_thresholding(log_m, lmax_m, float(threshold), remove_duplicate=True)
    return spots, float(threshold), note
```
This is what `scripts/experiments/03_bigfish_sweep.py` uses on the real image. Filtering on the full image and
zeroing afterwards avoids the strong LoG ring that an artificial step at the mask border would create. Spots whose
centre is within ~2·sigma of a *physical* edge (chamber rim, saturated area) are still biased and must be flagged
`near_edge`.

### 1.5 `decompose_dense` (dense-region decomposition)
```python
det.decompose_dense(image, spots, voxel_size, spot_radius, kernel_size=None, alpha=0.5, beta=1, gamma=5)
```
* Has **no pixel-only form**: pass `voxel_size=1, spot_radius=sigma_px` (ratio = px) and `kernel_size` explicitly
  in px (background sigma) or leave it (= `gamma*spot_radius`).
* Algorithm: subtract a Gaussian background; build the **median reference spot** from detected spots (`alpha=0.5`),
  fit a symmetric Gaussian to it; every connected region above `beta * max(reference spot)` is treated as a dense
  cluster and refilled with as many copies of the reference Gaussian as its integrated intensity allows.
* Model assumption: all spots are identical, diffraction-limited Gaussians, and brightness adds. **Polonies violate
  this**: they are growth colonies with a wide brightness range (amplitude p10–p90 spans ~16× on the test image),
  are often elongated/irregular, and a polony brighter than the median is one object, not several. Decomposition
  will therefore split bright single polonies into multiple "spots". Keep `dense_decomposition=False` (see §2).
* **dtype bug (verified):** with a `float32` image, `decompose_dense` crashes as soon as one dense region exists:
  `_gaussian_mixture_2d` calls `np.iinfo(image.dtype)` → `ValueError: Invalid integer data type 'f'`. It only
  works on `uint8/uint16`, i.e. you must re-quantise the background-corrected image (`np.clip(np.rint(x),0,65535).astype(uint16)`).
* It operates on the whole image you give it (no mask) and runs its own Gaussian background subtraction
  (`gamma*spot_radius` = 25 px at sigma 5) on top of ours.

### 1.6 Other verified facts
* `bigfish.__version__ == "0.6.2"`; `detect_spots(..., return_threshold=True)` returns `(spots, threshold)`.
* `automated_threshold_setting(image, mask_local_max)` uses `percentile(image.ravel(), 99.9999)` — zeros outside the
  mask are harmless (they are not local maxima above any threshold > 0).
* `spots_thresholding(image, mask, threshold=None)` returns zero spots with a `UserWarning` (does not raise).
* Big-FISH gives no per-spot intensity, background, SNR or size. The engine must measure those itself on the
  background-corrected image (peak = max in a radius-`sigma` disc; background = median of an annulus
  `[3σ, 4σ]`; SNR = (peak − bg)/std(annulus)); `saturated` must be read from the **raw** image (== dtype max).

### 2.1 What is actually in the TIFF (important for Core/ingest)
* 2 pages uint16 2450×1438. ImageJ metadata says `slices=2` (stored as **z-slices, not channels**), `unit=cm`,

### 2.2 Spot size and Big-FISH parameters (script 03; scored inside the 17 counted cells, match tolerance 15 px)
* The manual clicks are imprecise: distance from a mark to the nearest LoG maximum: median 6.5 px, p75 9.5,
  p90 11.5 px. Hence scoring uses 15 px (≈ visible polony radius). At 8 px every config looks ~15 points worse.
* LoG sigma (`spot_radius_px`): with Big-FISH auto threshold, sigma 3 → ~200–250 detections (prec ≈ 0.2);
  sigma 5 → ~90 (recall 0.56, prec 0.47); sigma 8 → ~45 (recall 0.40, prec 0.70). Best "oracle" F1 per config
  is only 0.5–0.63: **LoG intensity alone cannot separate polonies from debris on this image** (2.4).
* `minimum_distance` = 1× vs 2× sigma changed counts by ≤ 4 % — not a sensitive parameter in this density range.
* Background correction (sigma 5, auto threshold): none 106 det / gauss15 88 / gauss25 94 / gauss50 91 /
  gauss100 91 / masked-gauss50 94 / tophat10 96 / tophat20 106; recall 0.55–0.57 for all. **The choice of
  background method barely matters inside the chamber** once the analysis is masked; what matters is masking (2.3).

### 2.3 Bright edges, rim and saturation (script 04)
* LoG on raw page 0 (sigma 5): saturated plateau core median response 4.5 (flat ⇒ ~0), but **p99 = 608 along
  saturation boundaries vs p99 = 53 inside the chamber**; 1882 local maxima sit in the saturation-edge band.
  Saturation plateaus therefore do not produce spots in their interior but produce a dense chain of strong
  false spots along their border.
* **Unmasked whole-image** detection (gauss50, sigma 5, auto): 461 spots, **371 outside the chamber**, 393 within
  3 px of saturated pixels, only 54 in the counted cells; the auto threshold is pushed up to 93 (vs 41 when
  masked), losing faint polonies. With no background correction: 1922 spots (1488 outside). Masked-Gaussian
  background (saturated pixels excluded from the estimate) did not help unmasked detection (1193 spots).
  ⇒ **A counting region that excludes the rim and the outside is mandatory before thresholding**, and the
  automated threshold must be computed only from the masked LoG (snippet §1.4).

### 2.4 Debris ("dust") is the dominant false positive (script 06)
* Unmatched detections are tiny, very bright specks (sigma ≈ 1–3 px, often near-saturated) visible all over the
  chamber; the researcher did not mark them. Scale-selection ratio
  `max(σ²·LoG at σ=1.5,2) / max(σ²·LoG at σ=5,7)` (5×5 window around the detection):
  **manual marks median 0.34 (p90 0.66); unmatched detections median 6.7–7.8; matched 0.46**.
* Sigma 5, gauss50, masked: auto threshold (41): 91 det, R 0.56, P 0.47.
  + reject ratio ≥ 1.0: 47 det, R 0.55, **P 0.89**.
  Threshold auto×0.5: 141 → 58 after filter, R 0.69, P 0.91 (F1 0.79).
  Threshold auto×0.3: 191 → 66 after filter, **R 0.78, P 0.91 (F1 0.84)**.
  (Tuned and scored on the same image; treat as indicative only.)
* Remaining misses (viewed in `06_size_filter_crop.png`): faint diffuse polonies, and a faint polony touching a
  bright one (merged into one LoG peak). Remaining false positives: rim artefacts where counted cells touch the rim.

### 2.5 Dense decomposition (script 04) — harmful here
* On top of the masked sigma-5 detection (91 spots, 43 matched), `decompose_dense` (uint16 input, beta=1):
  **439 spots (+348), 169 "dense regions", matched marks fell to 13**; beta=2: 305 spots. The median
  reference spot (max 13111) is a bright polony; every polony brighter than it, plus rim and worm-like
  artefacts, becomes a "cluster" and is filled with several Gaussians (overlay `04_dense_overlay.png`: stacks of
  spots on single bright polonies). Polonies are not identical diffraction-limited spots, so the model does not
  hold. **Keep `dense_decomposition=False`; do not expose it as a recommended option.** Touching polonies should be
  split manually (the `split` action).

### 2.6 Cross-channel facts (script 05)
* Phase correlation page1→page0: whole image (0.5, −0.9) px (dy, dx); on log-gradient (1.2, −0.9); interior crop
  (−2.8, −0.7). skimage reports error ≈ 1.0 for all: **the estimate is unreliable** because the two pages share
  little structure except the chamber rim and debris. Conclusion: residual shift is ~1 px from the rim; do
  not auto-apply; show it.
* Bleed-through page0→page1 at the marked polonies: ratio median **0.0004** (p90 0.0075), LSQ slope 0.0003:
  negligible red→green crosstalk on this scan.
* Page 1 auto detection (masked, sigma 5): 37 spots; **21 of them are within 5 px of a page-0 detection — and
  none of these 21 is a marked polony**. They are compact (moment sigma 0.8–4.7 px) and bright/saturated in both
  pages: broadband-fluorescent debris. **On this image every would-be "yellow" object is debris.** Pair
  offsets: median 3.2 px (p10 1.0 px) — includes 1 px integer truncation per channel.
* Spacing: nearest-neighbour distance between manual marks min 14.6 px, p5 24.3 px, median 57.7 px; between
  kept detections min 18.7 px, p5 36 px.

## 3. Recommended defaults

| Parameter | Recommended | Contract default | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `spot_radius_px` (→ Big-FISH `log_kernel_size`, a **sigma**) | **5.0** for scans like test_img; **estimate per slide** | 4.0 | fitted sigma median 5.3 px; screenshots suggest 8–12 on other slides |
| `minimum_distance_px` | **= spot_radius_px (5)** | 6.0 | insensitive (≤4 %); keep ≤ half the minimum polony spacing (~15 px) |
| `background.method` / `sigma_px` | **gaussian_subtract, 50 px** (≈10× sigma) | same | 15–100 all equivalent inside the mask; tophat no better and slower |
| `threshold` | None (auto on **masked** LoG), then user adjusts | None | auto was ~2–3× too strict vs manual marks here; see below |
| `threshold_scale` | 1.0 by default; offer a slider 0.2–2 with live preview | 1.0 | best agreement at 0.3 **only together with a debris filter** |
| `dense_decomposition` | **False, hide or mark "not for polonies"** | False | harmful (2.5) |
| `match_tolerance_px` | **6 px at sigma 5** (≈1.2 × spot_radius_px, ≤ 0.4 × minimum spacing) after applying any confirmed shift | 5.0 | registration residual ~1–3 px + integer centroids; min polony spacing 14.6 px |
| `remove_duplicate_radius_px` | 3.0 fine | 3.0 | |

Estimating `spot_radius_px` from the data (recommended "estimate" button): take the 20–50 brightest *non-compact*,
non-saturated, non-edge detections from a first pass (sigma 5), fit a 2-D Gaussian (or second moments of the
background-subtracted 41×41 patch) and use the median sigma (clamped 2–15 px). Alternatively let the user click
3–5 typical polonies and fit those. Script 02 does exactly this.

**Debris filter (strongly recommended; not part of the original contract):**
compute per detection the scale ratio of §2.4. If ratio ≥ 1.0 (peak scale < ~3 px), the object is compact
debris. Recommended behaviour: do not count it as red/green/yellow; keep it in a separate "rejected candidates"
layer (or as `uncertain` with `suspicious_shape` only if the user opts in), so it is auditable and restorable.
Without it, ~50 % of auto detections on this image are debris and all would-be yellows are debris.

Other measured review reasons that the engine can compute cheaply:
* `saturated`: any raw pixel == 65535 within sigma of the spot (red and green separately).
* `near_edge`: within 2·sigma of the counting-region border or of a saturated / exclusion pixel.
* `near_threshold`: LoG value at the spot < 1.5 × threshold used.
* `weak_signal`: SNR (peak−bg)/std(annulus) < 3.
* `suspicious_shape`: scale ratio ≥ 1.0 (compact) or elongation (moment eigenvalue ratio) > 3 (worm artefacts).

### What is and is not validated
State after the single-image experiments of §2 (the 8-slide results are in [REAL_DATA_VALIDATION.md](REAL_DATA_VALIDATION.md) and [ALL_SLIDES_EVAL.md](ALL_SLIDES_EVAL.md)).
* Validated (one image, one annotator): polony sigma on this scanner/slide ≈ 5 px; masking is essential; background
  method is not critical inside the mask; dense decomposition is harmful; debris is the main false-positive source
  and is separable by scale; red→green bleed-through is negligible on this scan.
* **Not validated:** any accuracy/recall number on other slides, any yellow/green detection performance (the
  only real image has no green polonies), the 10 µm/px calibration, threshold behaviour on dense plates like the
  screenshots, the chamber/rim behaviour of other slides. No synthetic data were used for accuracy statements.

## 4. Association & yellow-logic review (CONTRACTS `associate`)

The Hungarian one-to-one assignment gated by a tolerance, with ambiguity → `uncertain`, is the right design.
Points to get right:

1. **Registration first.** Use `estimate_shift` only as a suggestion. On test_img it is unreliable (error ≈ 1.0)
   because channels share little structure. Better: estimate the shift from **co-localised compact debris** (bright
   in both channels — exactly what scripts 05 found: 21 pairs, median offset 3.2 px) — a robust median of
   pair offsets within 10 px is a good "registration check". Apply only integer shifts the user confirms
   (contract) and record them in provenance.
2. **Tolerance.** Must exceed (registration residual + localisation error of both channels) and stay well below the
   minimum spacing of distinct polonies. Measured: residual ~1–3 px, integer-truncated Big-FISH centroids (±1 px
   each), polony sigma 5, min spacing 14.6 px ⇒ **6 px** (1.2 × sigma). Scale it with `spot_radius_px` if the
   user changes the latter. Use sub-pixel refined positions (e.g. intensity-weighted centroid) for matching, not
   the truncated Big-FISH integers.
3. **Ambiguity margin.** Mark `ambiguous_match` when a spot has a second candidate within the tolerance whose
   distance is within 2 px (or 30 %) of the chosen one — e.g. a red polony between two green ones.
4. **False yellow from debris.** Broadband fluorescent debris is bright in both channels and compact. Run the
   debris filter **before** association, per channel. A yellow whose two evidences are both compact or both
   saturated must not be counted automatically.
5. **False yellow from adjacent polonies.** A red and a green polony touching (~15–25 px apart) can fall inside a
   generous tolerance. Keep the tolerance ≤ 0.4 × minimum spacing and show both channel positions in review.
6. **Bleed-through.** Negligible on test_img (0.04 % red→green). Not measured green→red (no green polonies). A
   bright polony in one channel can still create a faint local maximum in the other at higher PMT; require that the
   secondary-channel evidence exceed `k ×` the bleed-through prediction: `green_net > max(3·noise, 5 × slope ×
   red_net)`, where slope is measured per slide (procedure below).
7. **Below-threshold partner.** A real dual-positive polony that is weak in one channel is detected only in the
   other and becomes red-only/green-only. Recommended: for every unmatched spot, **measure** the other channel at
   the same (shifted) position (net signal and SNR, no detection). If SNR ≥ 3 but no spot was detected → add
   `near_threshold` and keep the class but surface it in the review queue (do not auto-convert to yellow).
   Store this measured evidence in the other channel's `ChannelEvidence` with `detected=False`.
8. **Sensitivity differences.** Channels have different PMT/laser settings and dyes: never use one threshold for both
   (Big-FISH list input would). Per-channel auto thresholds and per-channel `threshold_scale`.
9. **Object position.** Mean of matched positions is fine; also keep both raw positions (already in evidence).

**Bleed-through / registration sanity check the app can offer (one button, per slide):**
1. Detect (masked) in both channels with current parameters.
2. Pairs within 10 px whose both members are compact or saturated → "debris pairs". If ≥ 5: report median
   (dy, dx) and MAD. If |median| ≥ 1 px offer to apply the rounded shift.
3. For all red-only detections (non-saturated), regress green net signal on red net signal at the same positions
   (through the origin, robust); same for green→red. Report slopes (expected < 0.01; test_img 0.0003). Warn if
   > 0.05 (possible filter/crosstalk problem) and include the slope in provenance.
4. Report the count of yellow candidates that are compact/saturated, and the distribution of association
   distances (a healthy distribution is concentrated well below the tolerance; a flat one up to the tolerance
   means the tolerance is too large or the shift is wrong).

## 5. Researcher CSVs and unresolved scientific questions

### 5.1 Verified CSV facts (script 07, both files, 24 rows each, error < 4e-10)
`FAGE ABUNDENCE [fage mL-1] = mean(non-blank field cells) × (field / grid) / SAMPLE VOLUM[ul] × 1000`
= `mean × 2.9/0.1 / 2.5 × 1000` = **mean × 11600**.
* Blank cells are excluded from the mean, zeros included (blank = "field not counted", 0 = "counted, none") —
  the app's `CsvFieldCell` None vs 0 distinction is essential. No row has blanks inside the run (only trailing).
* Number of counted fields varies per row (6–24).
* `CONCENTRATION FACTOR` = 1 everywhere, so the CSV does not reveal whether it multiplies or divides.
* `QC = 0` for PI_A, PI_B in T7B (and their abundance is still computed); meaning unknown.
* With the legacy 5 µL the factor would be 5800 (half). The present files use 2.5 µL.
* `field [Pixel^2] = 2.9`, `grid [Pixel^2] = 0.1` are almost certainly **cm²**, not pixel²: the embedded grid
  in test_img is 316.23 px = 0.316 cm at 1000 px/cm ⇒ 0.100 cm², and the chamber is ≈ 2.8 cm². So "field" in the
  header = whole chamber (gel) area, "grid" = one counted square, and "field 1..24" columns are actually grid squares.
* Test_img manual marks: 77 in 17 squares ⇒ 4.53 per square ⇒ 52,541 mL⁻¹ by the same formula (not to be
  compared with the CSV rows: unknown which slide this is).

### 5.2 Questions for the researcher (blockers for any concentration output)
1. What are **T7A and T7B**? Two phages/targets, two dyes/channels (which is red, which green?), or two
   replicate plates? The app must not map them to colours by itself (`csv_color_mapping` user-set only).
2. Is the **10 µm/px** from the TIFF resolution tag the true scanner pixel size? Is `field = 2.9` the chamber area
   in cm², measured or nominal? Does the 2.5 µL spread over exactly that area?
3. Grid geometry: the overlay has 4 × 7 = 28 squares (numbering 0–27 in ImageJ?) but the CSV has fields 1–24 and
   test_img has 17 counted squares (counters 0–16). Which squares are eligible (fully inside the chamber? not on
   the rim? not in the dark crescent?) and in what order are they numbered? Are partial squares counted?
4. Is a **blank** field "not counted/not valid" (excluded) and **0** a real zero? (This is how the formula treats
   them.) What decides when to stop counting fields?
5. What does **QC = 0** mean, and should such rows be excluded from summaries?
6. **Concentration factor**: multiply or divide? Applied before or after the volume?
7. The legacy **5 µL hardcode**: which historical files used 5 µL? (Old abundances would be 2× off if re-computed
   with 2.5.)
8. How should **yellow** (dual-positive) polonies count in T7A/T7B: in both, in neither, separately?
   (This is `reporting_mode` exclusive vs channel_inclusive.)
9. Are compact very bright specks always debris, or can a real polony be that small (early, small colonies)?
   Is there a minimum polony size criterion?
10. Are polonies in the dark crescent / near the rim / on worm-like artefacts counted?
11. Should mean-per-square extrapolation be replaced by count/total-counted-area (identical when all squares are
    equal and fully inside the chamber; different for partial squares)?
12. Which page is which channel when the file only says `635 [W1]` once? (Page 0 = 635 nm red here, page 1 had no
    polonies, so the mapping could not be confirmed from this image; the 8-slide data give strong evidence, see REAL_DATA_VALIDATION.md stage 1.)
